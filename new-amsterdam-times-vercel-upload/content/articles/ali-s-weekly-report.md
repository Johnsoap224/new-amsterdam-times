## Week of October 26–30, 2026

Numbers as of: Friday, October 30, 2026, 4:00 p.m. ET

The 30-second version: Stocks fell for a third straight week, S&P -0.6% and Nasdaq -2.0%. Alphabet, Amazon, and Intel were all punished for AI Capital Expenditures (capex) with no visible return. 9 of 11 sectors rose and small caps gained, so money rotated rather than left. Economic indicators showed a “run hot” economy. GDP came in at 3.4%, the Fed held but essentially guaranteed a hike in the December meeting. Treasury yields fell as investors fled to safety, the 10yr is finally below 5.0% at 4.94%. Oil climbed a fourth straight week to $104 on Iran risk, and the asset that is supposed to protect you broke, gold hit a one year low (I have zero clue why this happened). This week look out for midterm results on Tuesday, Jobs report on Friday, and Iran any day (hopeful news as of 11/2).

Biggest winner this week: The only major index that finished in the green was the Russell 2000, +1.3%

This on the surface is odd, in a regular selloff that should not happen. Small caps, which make up the Russell 2000 sit further out on the risk curve (below is a super simple risk curve), generally further than big tech. the risk curve is essentially high risk high reward. Bonds and cash equivalents are usually low risk, so the return isn’t as great. So, when investors get scared, the Russell usually takes a hard hit as they rotate their money to safer investments. It rose in this case because it wasn’t a flight from risk, it was a flight from one trade. The Russell holds regional banks, industrials, energy and small biotech AKA they hold none of the AI mega caps we hear about day to day. As you read earlier companies like Alphabet and Amazon were hit hard, which hurt the S&Ps returns. Money moved sideways across the curve from one risky asset to another rather than inward towards safety. It’s also worth noting that AI companies saw their bonds move further out the risk curve as credit spreads widen given their recent earnings report.

Biggest loser this week: Lot of losers this week, we will be highlighting the Nasdaq which went 2.0%

The Nasdaq posted a third straight weekly loss, and a -5.3% October, its worst month since March 2025. The Nasdaq is cap weighted which means the larger the market cap a company has the more that company can affect the index. 9 out of the 10 largest cap companies listed on the Nasdaq are directly involved in the AI buildout. These 9 companies make up 56% of the index. There are 3,000 companies listed on the Nasdaq composite. Yes 9 companies make up more of the index than 2,990 others. Given that this index is so tech heavy Amazons losses, Alphabet losses, Intel’s losses all live here. The Nasdaq’s fear gauge, the VXN closed at 28.5 while the broader market VIX sat at 19.4. The larger the number the more volatile the market is. That gap shows us the fear lives in tech, not the whole market. This is the AI trade getting pushed further out the risk curve. AI mega caps that had been trading like safe assets got repriced as what they are: companies spending a shit ton of money on an uncertain payoff. Money rotated to things like healthcare and treasury bonds. Hence, the line of the week. Show Me the Money.

A booming Economy and a falling Market at the same time

What happened: The S&P fell 0.6% and the Nasdaq 2.0%, a third straight weekly loss for both. In the same 5 days Q3 GDP came in at 3.4% vs 3.2% expected, consumer jumped to 91.7, jobless claims fell to 194k, and the Fed held rates while all but pointing to a December hike. Treasury yields fell anyway, the 10yr to 4.94%, oil rose to $104. Gold fell 3% to a one year low.

Why the market cared: Normally a strong economy and a strong stock market go together. Last week they split, and every market was reacting to the same two facts: the economy is running hot, so the Fed keeps hiking and rates stay near 5%

Stocks. The selling was narrow. Alphabet fell 12.6% on negative FCF and higher projected capex for next year: Microsoft rose 3.0% on positive FCF. 9 of 11 sectors rose. Money didn’t leave the market, it moved from the AI spenders to everything else, we have seen gains in healthcare lead by Eli Lilly, materials, utilities, industrials, and many more. Pick and Shovel companies linked to AI have performed exceptionally well. Think the guys supplying the Alphabets and Microsoft so GE Vernova, SK Hynix, copper miners. SK Hynix posted a record quarter with memory chips sold out through 2029, and GE Vernova, Vertiv and Corning all reported surging orders for the power, cooling and fiber that data centers need. Nvidia was the exception, down 3% with the rest of mega-cap tech. The Nasdaq fell three times as hard as the S&P because nine companies are more than half of it and there’s almost nothing in it to rotate into.

Rates. Treasury yields fell even though the Fed is leaning toward a hike. The 10yr is down to 4.94% from 5.00% and the 2yr down to 4.56% from 4.69%. Two things drove this. First, the money that didn’t rotate into other stocks went to Treasury bonds. This pushes bond prices up and yields down. I recommend looking into how yields work, it’s a cornerstone of the financial markets. Second, Fed Chair Warsh said at Wednesday’s press conference that high yields are already doing some of the Fed’s work. This made traders trim how many more hikes are coming. That’s why the 2yr which tracks fed expectations fell twice as much. That is called a bull steepener. We can talk about this more if you are curious. December is still about 80% priced in through fed funds futures (contracts that bet on where the Fed’s rate will be). One caution from the Sevens Report: this was a fear-driven drop in yields, not an inflation-driven

one, core PCE inflation ticked up to 3.1%, which is why lower yields didn’t lift stocks the way they usually do. So, Stocks went down Treasury bonds went up.

Credit. The AI story is showing up in credit markets too. The cost of insuring the debt of AI linked borrowers like Meta and Oracle against default which is measured by CDS (Credit Default Swap) spreads kept rising last week according to Essaye. Yardeni notes that credit spreads are widening for AI borrowers generally. A spread is the extra yield a company has to bay over a Treasury bond to borrow. When it widens, lenders are saying this got riskier so pay us more. Essaye is saying investors are paying more to protect against an AI default. Yardeni is saying AI companies are paying more to borrow. The message is the same lenders want more to fund AI capex. That matters because spread is the cost of the money. Every 20 bps of widening adds $20 million a year to the interest on $10 billion of bonds, for the life of the bonds, and the hyperscalers are planning hundreds of billions of this. It’s worth noting the 10-year Treasury yield fell 6 bps last week, but if an AI borrower’s spread widened by more than that, its actual borrowing cost went up while the “risk-free” rate went down. Lower Treasury yields don’t help you if lenders have decided you’re riskier. You can already see companies routing around it. Meta raised $95 billion by selling stock, not bonds. SpaceX financed $40 billion of chips and servers with asset-backed deals. Anthropic filed to go public. Yardeni’s believes that AI funding is shifting from corporate bonds toward equity, private credit and asset-backed structures. “The Bond Vigilantes may be growing more discerning, but the financing toolkit continues to expand.”

That is the optimistic version: the buildout keeps getting funded. The cautious version from the Essaye: AI credit worry is now one of the main things pushing money into Treasuries, and it’s not going away.

Essaye and Yardeni are 2 guys whose stuff I read

Commodities. Oil rose a fourth straight week, the WTI increased to $104.12, Brent finished in the $113-117 range. Nothing was cut from supply. The price is a risk premium, a bet that the US resumes strikes on Iran right after midterm elections, reinforced by a WSJ report Wednesday. Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure pushed oil up. An analyst I read thinks the trade is lopsided, a strike is priced in, a deal would hit oil hard. Mondays drop in brent to under $109 on weekend talks tells us how fast that goes. This is pretty important as oil above $100 is a driver of headline inflation

Volatility. The VIX (Cboe Volatility Index, the fear gauge built from S&P 500 options) spiked to

23.9 during Friday’s selloff but closed at 19.4, almost exactly where it started the week. The Nasdaq’s version, the VXN, closed at 28.5. That gap showed investors feared tech, not of the market. On the worst day since June, the price of crash insurance never reached “crisis.”

Currencies. The dollar index sat flat around 101.9. A scary week normally lifts the dollar as a safe haven; it didn’t, which is one more sign the selling was about AI, not about the US economy. The Brazilian real fell 8% in a single day on Lula’s surprise win. A reminder, with the US midterms Tuesday, that elections can move markets fast.
