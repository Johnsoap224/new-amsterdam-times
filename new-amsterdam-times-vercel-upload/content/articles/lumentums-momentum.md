Thanks for coming back to the Newsletter

This week we are talking about Lumentum Holdings (LITE). Formed in 2015 as a spin-off from JDS Uniphase, this San Jose based company manufactures optical transceivers and photonic chips currently being used to in AI data centers to help speedily transfer data. Because of AI’s boom, this company has grown a lot in the last year.

This company has come back from red. In 2024 its revenue shrunk by 23%. Now, according to analyst consensus, the company’s revenue it is expected to grow by 2.09x in 2027. This comes even after 2026 being great for Lumentum. While I would wait until some of next year’s quarter reports come in, but I’m optimistic.

Its Gross Margin is up 13.7 points from last year and 8.4 points from its 5-year average, including its bad year of 2024. Its EPS grew by 4.21x compared to last year sitting at about $8.67. Compared to the other companies in its sector this EPS is on average 5x better.

Currently Lumentum’s Debt/EBITDA ratio is 2.07 with the company’s debt declining by 36% compared to 2025. However, this is not due to regular deleveraging practices. The company has issued $1.265B in new 2032 convertible notes. Also, $519.4M of note principal was settled through conversions or paying off debt by giving stock to creditors. This among other things has made the company’s Common Shares outstanding rise from 69.8M to 88.6M. While the balance sheet is in better shape and is a lot less leveraged, this has come at a cost of serious dilution.

Another thing to keep in mind for Lumentum would be how its revenues are broken down. With nearly 42% of its 2026 revenue coming from its largest two customers, this company doesn’t have a lot when it comes to diversity of sales. This could pose a risk, especially if you have concerns about the AI loop. The risk could already be showing as currently Lumentum is seeing some serious problems with its Accounts Receivable and Inventory. Compared to last year its Accounts Receivable increased by 208% and its Inventory increased by 147%. In the future, when you are looking at Lumentum, I would keep a watch on these indicators.

Overall, my main concern is with the company’s Capex, which has nearly doubled from last year to $451M. This year Lumentum’s Free Cash Flow was positive for the first time in the last 3 years, but a big chunk of this cash was eaten up by this giant Capex. So unless this trend reverses, I would be wary.

The make it break it point for this company is how much can it capitalize on AI and will they be able to not sink the ship. recently on September 15th, Lumentum announced at they will be attending ECOC (European Conference on Optical Communications) where the panel is expected to discuss their 800G and 1.6T silicon-photonic transmitters, AI interconnect lasers, optical networking products, and VCSEL arrays. In addition, the company’s CEO, Michael Hurlson, has recently shared his confidence in Lumentum being able to stay relatively insulated from data center concerns and weakness, with him discussing how the company could capitalize more from the current AI driven demand and the recent expansion of US manufacturing. Could this be a step in the right direction? It feels like it could.

Overall, I would say if you wanted to invest into many aspects of the AI supply chain, this might be a company you could ride the wave on. The stock has performed well, but there are concerns about its CapEx and potential over expansion. I would keep an eye on how the company works, and certainly do your own research.
