NVIDIA Report What Happened: On November 9, Nvidia released that Jensen Huang, the president, CEO, and board member of the company, had reportedly been having two extramarital affairs. One of those affairs was a woman who U.S. intelligence agencies suspect of being a Chinese spy, who was suspected of having previously passed information to the Chinese government. Nvidia’s board announced an independent investigation in Huang’s conduct, announcing that at this time there’s no reason to believe that national security or company secrets had been compromised. On November 12, Jensen Huang resigned from his positions, triggering Colette Kress, the company’s finance chief, to step into the role of interim CEO. The company has since stated that they’re actively looking for someone to fill the role full-time. Additionally, Harvey C. Jones, former CEO of Synopsys, has filled the open board position. Market Impact: The recent market was already in a bear position, with questions about data center production and AI revenue doubts pulling back hard on both the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ 100 indexes. Wednesday afternoon, Nvidia closed at $173.73 per share. Overnight, the stock price crashed nearly 13%. It has since recovered to $166.19 per share. Company Fundamentals: While leadership has an impact, internal issues don’t change the revenue, margin, demand, and production trends that Nvidia has continued to produce. In the second quarter of fiscal 2027, Nvidia reported a revenue boost of 106% from the previous year, and data center revenue jumped 177% since the previous year. Management has also indicated that it expects roughly 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, almost double the growth that analysts had been modeling. Should I Invest? There’s no real way of knowing how much NVDA stock will dip or recover. There are some large companies that have gone through similar instances in the past that can serve as key indicators of Nvidia’s future. Take McDonald’s and Intel for instance. In November of 2019, Steve Easterbrook, McDonald’s CEO, was caught having a relationship with a company employee. The stock grew roughly 80% over the next few years to its all-time high. Intel’s CEO Brian Krzanich had a relationship with an employee as well in June 2018. Since then, INTC surged to a record high in 2026, growing 200% in 8 years.

To be clear, this does not mean that NVDA’s stock price will flip overnight. If you decide to buy the dip in Nvidia, this could be a strong long term decision based on past history and company research specific to Nvidia. The dip and bear market very likely will continue for weeks to come, but Nvidia is a strong long-term play for anyone looking to buy and hold for 5 or even 10+ years. Another factor to consider is the timing of Nvidia’s earnings report. Recently, AI and tech sectors, including the semiconductor sector, have struggled to meet growing expectations. Investors are looking for over-the-top reports in addition to optimistic news about growing chip demand. This environment makes it tough for companies to meet earnings goals, making it unlikely that Nvidia’s earning report next Tuesday will satisfy investors, especially after this incident. Considering the market environment, the strongest option would be implementing a dollar cost average* approach if you’re interested in buying the dip.
