GEV article

How are all these new AI systems being powered?

In 2024, General Electric was going through a breakup. The company had gotten too big, and it was time to divide. GE split into three distinct companies. GE Vernova was the company that got all the products you would usually associate with GE GE Power, GE Renewable Energy, GE Digital, and GE Energy Financial Services. This Massachusetts based company is now feeling the boom in AI and may continue to grow so long as AI grows. But enough about that let’s get into the specifics.

For 2026, GEV is expected to earn more than $45.5 Billion, a 19.4% YoY increase from last year’s $38.1 Billion. Because the company is new, it is hard to estimate how much each part of GEV’s business earned when it was part of GE. However, estimates generally ranged from $29.65 Billion to $33.24 Billion. Because of this, most of the enormous growth over the past year can be attributed to new AI business.

Gross margin has steadily risen, even when we account for reasonable estimates for years before 2024. The second quarter of 2026 added a full percentage point on its own. The current trailing 12 month gross margin is 20.6% and would be higher if not for losses in wind energy. So, for those who think green energy will bring the next boom, it seems the market says otherwise.

The company’s first half of 2026 was strong, especially when you look at its GAAP EPS, which was $19.96. While $4.4 Billion of these gains came from selling company assets, remember that this is only the first half of the fiscal year. In 2025, EPS was $17.69, although that figure can be attributed to a $2.9 Billion tax benefit. In short, the company’s EPS is still growing and doing relatively well.

Despite this growth and an acquisition, the company does not have much debt. As of June 30th, 2026, it had around $3.73 Billion in debt, giving it a debt to EBITDA ratio of just 0.9x. This suggests the company does not pose too much risk. Unlike at other companies benefiting from the AI boom, investors may not see this debt as a future burden that could lower the stock price.

GEV’s free cash flow has been slowly increasing, rising by $1.26 Billion in 2024 and another $2.01 Billion in 2025. In 2025, the company’s free cash flow was $3.71 Billion, with an FCF margin of 9.7%. But hold on: in 2025, the company’s operating cash flow was $4.99 Billion, while in the first half of 2026, operating cash flow was $10.68 Billion and free cash flow was $9.9 Billion. Much of this cash came from contracts that have not yet been completed. Still, these figures are strong indicators of the company’s health.

GEV’s outlook is positive. It has grown a lot over the past year and has many open contracts that will carry into the future. Although its debt increased after the acquisition of Prolec GE, the company still has plenty of cash, and its FCF suggests that this is unlikely to change. Overall, I would say this is a safe bet. Even if the AI market suddenly disappeared tomorrow, the company would not disappear with it. The main concern is its green energy division, which is still losing money. So, if you hear that the company may close it down, don’t panic you might even consider buying the stock.
