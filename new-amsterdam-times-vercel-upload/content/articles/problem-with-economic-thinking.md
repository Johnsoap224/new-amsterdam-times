If you have taken an Economics class or have listened to someone who describes themselves as an economic thinker, you have probably been told that they try to look at the world through an unbiased lens and instead just measure the world by data and economic theory derived from data. They will then give examples of how a certain economic policy would work in our world with comical examples, strung out to avoid the most amount of offense, watered down of all substance of the political so that by the end, the example is meaningless and is more metaphorical.

Economist will go far and beyond to show that they are unbiased, and whenever you point out a bias that they might have, they will quickly try to hide behind the phrase, “all else being equal”. This criticism of their behavior is not trying to oust them or to destroy their world view but rather is made to show the appropriate cynicism needed to understand the problems with said thinking. The problem is not about whether or not their theories are incorrect, but rather the scope in which economic thinking is applied to the world. As such, all criticisms are trying to compel people into expanding their thought past the economical.

The main problem with Economic thinking is: the theory asserts itself as the highest level of reasoning. “If only you had thought about things economically, then the best choice would have been decided.” The Economist assumes that economical concerns are the explanation for all social behaviors. This is false. Economics is one of the most important topics of being social but is not The Topic of being social.

Social Interaction, I define, as Political, with the ranges of social interaction fitting into said definition. Many define Politics as interactions between governments, and individuals who interact with a government. But this is not the full story. Even social interactions in High School between teenagers is political in nature. The same is true for every example between these two extremes, from the social games played by cliques of mean girls to The Great Game played on the world stage.

It is such that the most important social behavior is political, not economical. Interactions with others is the most important factor in determining what someone will do. It trickles down into economical behavior, as economical behavior is subservient to Social Interaction. The only reason why Economists have been able to come to this false claim of supremacy is because the current political order is controlled through economic action.

Once the truth of the political supremacy is realized it is much easier to deconstruct the world and, in turn, the reason why things occur. Why does a leader sell off vast amounts of land a civil liberty to a foreign country, even though his constituents would be greatly angered by the lost economic power? Because said foreign power might invade the country, killing the population and him. Why would a King restrict the sale of arms to other countries, even though such capital could bring many of his subjects out of poverty and increase the development of his country? Because said arms might be used against him. Why would a politician levy a tax on a certain commodity even though the ramification of said tax could upend an economy? Because such a tax may curry favor from a political group who he needs support from to win an election.

Economist may try to explain this through Social Cost, but this is a tack on to their way of thinking and diminishes the importance of Social Interaction. It is better to look at Economical Interaction as a branch of Social Interaction, because you will come to better conclusions of why events have happened.

This then explains a questions that Economists cannot answer: Why do people and groups want to succeed economically? Economists will list reasons, but said reasons can only be explained by someone’s wanting to be viewed favorably in a social light.

That being said, do not discount Economists, they have realized many great facts about one of the most important aspects of social life. Just be cynical about their biases, specifically their bias to their way of thinking. If you have to pick between Keynes, Friedman, Marx and Machiavelli, just remember: only one of these authors didn’t publish their work to the masses.
