Hello there, thanks for reading the first Newsletter. This is the first one I have ever written so please have a little patience with me. This week we are looking at HPE or Hewlett Packard Enterprise.

Many of you may know HP as a solid computer company in the PC world, but maybe not its software brother. In 2015, seeing the space of cloud computing grow, HP split its Enterprise databasing wing off into Hewlett Packard Enterprise in order to structure its business model to address how to compete in the cloud space. It mainly operates cloud servers, company networking, and data storage, all of which has been important pillars for the AI boom. So how is it doing?

So far the first three quarters have been great for HPE. The company’s revenue this year is expected to be upwards of $46.09 Billion. If you compare it to its average growth over the past few years of 8% predicting $37.25 Billion, then revenues has outperformed expectations by 23%.

HPE’s gross margin this year is 40.2%, which is 1.25x that of last years gross margin. So far the Earning Quality for the year is high but do note: this has been a Cinderella year for the company.

The Earnings Per Share (EPS) of the stock is also doing better, with the ratio being 1.06 when using GAAP standards, beating previous years. However, analyst believe that the coming 4th Quarter will make the EPS upwards of 1.12-1.22, according to GAAP standards.

If I had a little concern it would be about the company’s cash reserves. Last year HPE invested about $13 Billion into financing operations, making them only carry over $1 Billion into the current year. Due to the boom in revenue from AI-linked services however, the company has already changed its cash flow outlook for 2027, anticipating a carryover of $5 Billion. When it comes to HPE’s debt, they owe about $20.5 Billion. The Debt/EBITDA ratio is expected to be around 1.8. This metric has excited the company, who says they have over performed their own internal goal of a ratio sitting at 2.0. I

It seems as though last year they wrote the check, this year: HPE is cashing it.

This all could be expected to a company who’s space is benefitting greatly from the rise of AI. Much of the company’s growth has come at the sale of server space to AI companies who are looking to aggressively expand. Importantly HPE is already in a sector that makes it stand apart from other companies: Networking.

HPE already is poised for growth in the networking space with its subsidiary HPE Aruba Networking, and knows how to deal with the infrastructure associated with wireless networks. With AI requiring more and more interconnectivity to operate, and with those operations being high in data traffic, HPE might just be the company who could step up to the task. Also think of this: in 2030 6G will rollout, relying heavily on AI to maximize its performance. HPE’s Networking branch would be in a prime position to capitalize on that. In the future many gains can be expected from this sector of the company.

Compared to its main competitors Hewlett Packard Enterprise isn’t performing as well as them. Dell is by far doing the best, with Cisco Lenovo, IBM, and Oracle, having more revenue. But its Year-over-Year growth is actually right in the middle of these giants. In my opinion this just shows HPE has the most to gain from more booms in AI Enterprise.

Overall Hewlett Packard Enterprise has had a great year and expectations for Q4 are looking up. That being said, the company has a lot of room for growth and if you want to invest in the Enterprise sector this would be a solid choice.

Thank you all for reading my first Newsletter! See you next week.
